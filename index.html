The words this page uses

Cold. One try, with no calculator to check it.

Held-out. Problems kept back for the test. The AI never studied them.

Base and distilled. The AI before it studied, and the AI after.

Weights. The numbers inside the AI. They are what changes when it learns.

Harvest. The first step: the AI works through problems while the calculator checks each step.

Fail-wall. The problems it gets wrong. The wall moves when it starts to get some of them right.

Adopt or dissolve. Keep the new version, or drop it. A rule written in code decides.

Grant. A person's recorded yes, in their own words. Without it, nothing can be built on a kept version.

The seal. A frozen list of every file that could change a result. It was made before the test.

The model's hands. The programs that run the AI. They decide nothing. They only write records.

One round, six moves

Only verified work trains it. Only a person lets the change stand.

01 — HARVEST

It works, checked.

One step at a time. Exact arithmetic rules on each. A wrong step is never kept.

02 — BUILD

Only what verified.

Finished, verified working becomes an example. The rest is thrown away.

03 — TRAIN

On that, and nothing else.

A small add-on, trained on the verified file. Its fingerprint is on record.

04 — TEST COLD

Problems it never saw.

Before and after. One try, no checker, on problems it never studied.

05 — DECIDE

Adopt or dissolve.

Better, and no more confidently wrong, or the weights are dropped. Published either way.

06 — GRANT

A person's yes.

Adopted weights wait for a recorded yes. The loop cannot write its own.

Three walls it cannot pass

The reach of the check. The only check is exact arithmetic. Nothing is learned that arithmetic cannot rule on.

The size of the model. About a billion parameters. What a small adapter can hold is finite.

What it can finish. It is only taught working it completed under the checker.

When a round's lift falls below the bar, it is dissolved and the loop stops. That is the house full, not the build failing.

Where it fits, and where it does not

Fits: work where a program can tell a right step from a wrong one. Calculations, reconciliations, conversions.

Does not fit: judgement, writing, anything with no exact check. With no check there is nothing verified to teach from.

Rolling it out

1 · Pick one kind of work with an exact check. Write the check first. 2 · Set a held-out block aside and never touch it. 3 · Run the checked process wide; keep only what verified. 4 · Train, test cold, let the rule decide. 5 · Name the person who grants a change. 6 · Stop when a round fails its bar.

Risks, answered straight

"Will it get worse?" It can. Then the rule drops the weights and the base stays. In round 2 they did not, and were dropped.

"Is it memorising?" In round 2, 0 of 229 held-out problems are in the training set and 0 were harvested.

"Does it still make confident mistakes?" Yes. In round 2: 113 of 229 after, 113 before. Keep the checker wherever a wrong answer costs you.

"Can it run away?" No. It cannot start from weights nobody granted, and it stops at a failed round, a round cap and an hours cap.

What this does — and doesn't — claim

One measured round is one measured round. 2 sealed rounds have run. This model, this kind of problem. Nothing about others.

The gain is not even. Right, before → after: one-step problems 6 → 6 of 6; several-step problems 108 → 108 of 153; the hardest problems 2 → 2 of 70. The hardest problems are ones like finding the two primes that multiply to a number, or a sum over a three-by-three grid.

A cold answer is not a checked answer. After training it still gave a confident wrong answer on 113 of 229 unseen problems.

How the round was run. Round 2 ran in 1 sitting. Every step ended well. The record.

The held-out problems are the same kinds of problem. New calculations from the same generator. The lift shows the skill carries to those, not elsewhere.

These are not The Climb's or The Crystal's numbers. Those run a 4-bit build in a browser tab with 128 tokens for one try. Here it is 32-bit on a laptop processor with 256, the same for both models.

The weights failed the plain hash check. The file's hash is not the maker's listed one. Every tensor was compared with a separate 8-bit copy instead, and all agree. How it was checked ↗

The trained weights are not published. Each adapter is recorded by its hash. Publishing weights derived from the model is a licence decision not yet taken.

A re-run may differ by a reply or two. Another processor can round a near-tie the other way. Checked: 100 of 100 replies identical to plain generation.

One test per round. An exact McNemar test at 0.05. Over several rounds the chance that one passes by luck adds up.

It is slow on a laptop. Round 2 took 5.9 hours of a laptop processor. The seal caps a round at 12 hours and all rounds at 48. If a round runs over, no further round may start.
