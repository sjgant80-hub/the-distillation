Built on The Climb and The Crystal · measured on a laptop

Taught only its own checked work.

A small AI model was held to a checker until every step of its working was right. Then it was trained on that verified working and on nothing else, and asked again cold: one try, no checker, on problems it had never seen. This page shows what changed, measured against claims written down first. The model's weights change only when the measurement says so and a person says yes.

Start here

The words this page uses. The base is the model a round starts from. The distilled model is the base after it has been trained on its own verified working. Cold means one try, with no checker and no second attempt. A held-out problem is one the model was never trained on and never worked through; the claims are measured only on those. A confident wrong answer is a reply that gives an answer and the answer is wrong (a reply that gives no answer is a failure too, but one that shows). The fail-wall is the set of problems a model cannot do cold; each square above is one held-out problem, coloured by which side of the wall it was on before and after. A round is one pass: harvest, build, train, test cold, decide. A claim's bar is the result it had to reach, written down before the round was run. Sealed means every file that can change a result was frozen first. Adopt means the trained weights are kept; dissolve means they are thrown away. A grant is a person's recorded yes to an adopted change.

How it works

A model trained on its own raw output gets worse: its mistakes become its lessons. The whole of this build is the filter in front of the training data, and the measurement after it.

Why held out

If the trained model were tested on the problems it trained on, a higher score would show only that it remembers them. So every round has a block of problems that is never harvested and never trained on, in any round: each round's held-out block is struck from every round's harvest pool before anything runs. Two problems count as the same problem when they are the same calculation, however they are worded. The held-out problems are new calculations of the same kinds as the training ones: this measures whether the skill carries to unseen problems of those kinds, not to other kinds of problem.

The loop: filling the house

One round is a unit. Run again from an adopted model, it harvests again, trains again and is measured again on a fresh held-out block. Each round is sealed and judged on its own, with the same six claims and the same bars. The gain from each round is expected to shrink, and when a round's lift falls below the bar it is dissolved and the loop stops. That is the house full, not the build failing: a model this size, with one kind of check, on a laptop, has only so much room.

The gate at the weights

Two gates stand between a trained model and the next round, and they are different on purpose. The rule is code: adopt only if the cold lift cleared its bar and the model gives no more confident wrong answers. The grant is a person: weights the rule adopted still wait for a recorded yes that names the round and the exact weights, by their SHA-256. The loop cannot write its own grant, and a grant for other weights opens nothing.

Three walls it cannot pass

  • The reach of the check. The only check is exact arithmetic. Nothing can be harvested, and so nothing learned, that arithmetic cannot rule on.
  • The size of the model. About a billion parameters. Each round adds a small adapter on top; what it can hold is finite.
  • The problems it can harvest. It can only be taught working it managed to finish under the checker. What it cannot finish even with the checker never reaches the training file.

To go further than the house allows means building a bigger house on purpose: another kind of check, a larger model, more compute. This loop will not do that by itself, and is built not to.

What this does not prove

  • One measured round is one measured round. not measured yet It says nothing about other models or other kinds of problem.
  • A cold answer is not a checked answer. not measured yet The second claim says only that it gives no more of them than before. An answer that must be right still needs the checker.
  • The held-out problems are the same kinds of problem. They are calculations the model never saw, made by the same generator as the training ones. A lift here shows the skill carries to new problems of these kinds. It does not show it carries anywhere else.
  • The problems are made by code. Word problems whose answer is an exact calculation. Nothing here measures messy, real questions.
  • These numbers are not The Climb's or The Crystal's numbers. Those pages run a 4-bit copy of the model in a browser tab and allow 128 tokens for one try. Here the model runs at full 32-bit precision on a laptop processor, and one try is allowed 256 tokens, because a verified working of a hard problem is long. Both models here get the same limit. The base model's cold score on this page should not be read against the one-try score on those pages.
  • The weights were checked, not taken on trust, and not against the maker's own hash. The file came from a public copy of the model. Its SHA-256 is not the one the maker lists (the copy was saved again by whoever mirrored it), so that comparison failed and is recorded as failed. Instead every tensor was compared with a separate 8-bit copy of the same model already on this machine, and all agree to within 8-bit rounding. SEAL.md has the method and the numbers.
  • The trained weights are not in this repository. Each adapter is recorded by its SHA-256, with the training file, the settings and the script that made it. Publishing weights derived from the model is a licence decision that has not been taken. Running the round yourself makes your own.
  • A re-run may differ by a reply or two. Replies are greedy and recorded, and every verdict is worked out from the records, which never change. But the same weights on another processor can round a near-tie the other way. The batched generation used here was checked against plain one-at-a-time generation: not measured yet
  • The development round shaped the settings. The token limits and the longest working that is trained on were chosen after a development round on other problems. Every problem that round touched is struck from every sealed held-out block. The two bars were fixed before its model was trained.
  • The significance test is one test. An exact two-sided McNemar test on the problems that changed sides. It says a difference this large is unlikely by chance on this block. It is not a promise about the next block.
  • Compute is the wall. not measured yet A graphics card would run it faster; it would not change what is measured.
  • Nothing here makes the model wiser about anything else. It learns to write this kind of working more reliably. It does not learn to reason in general, and nothing on this page claims it does.

How it is built

Seven small kernels decide everything, and each is mutation-gated: its tests must catch every small change to its code. The model's hands (the Python that loads weights, writes replies and trains) decide nothing; they write records, and the kernels judge the records.

not measured yet
  • src/split.mjs makes every block from its seed and strikes the held-out ones from every pool. A problem's identity is its calculation.
  • src/trace.mjs turns a finished crystal into a training example and verifies it from its own text: each line one step and exactly true, the answer the problem's own, and The Climb's sealed checker reading it the same way.
  • src/cold.mjs reads a cold reply as right, wrong or no answer with The Climb's sealed rule, compares the two models problem by problem, and refuses a cold record made on the wrong weights.
  • src/adopt.mjs is the rule (adopt or dissolve), the test of a person's grant, and the chain of weights a round may stand on.
  • src/rounds.mjs says what the loop may do next: run, wait for a person, stop because the house is full, or stop at a cap.
  • src/claims.mjs rules on the six claims from the evidence; with evidence missing a claim is pending, never passed.
  • src/judge.mjs takes a round's records and gives its verdict, and takes a record as evidence only when it was made as the seal requires. This page runs the same file when you press the button above.
  • vendor/crystal/ is The Crystal's sealed engine and, inside it, The Climb's sealed checker, byte for byte. The harvest is that engine run wide.
  • py/ is the model's hands: llm.py (load, generate), serve.py (a local door for the harvest), train.py (the LoRA fine-tune), cold.py (the cold test).
  • The seal (SEAL.json) is a SHA-256 of every file that can change a result: the kernels, the vendored engine, the Python, the scripts that harvest, build and judge, the settings and the claims. A sealed round records the seal it was made under. not measured yet
  • On every push, GitHub's own runners run the tests, the mutation gate on every kernel, the byte-for-byte check of the vendored engine, and decide every round again from its files.

For your business

What it is. A way to make a small model you own better at your own kind of work without sending anything out, without trusting the model's own output, and without the model changing by itself. not measured yet

Where it fits, and where it does not

It fits work where a program can tell a right step from a wrong one: calculations, reconciliations, conversions, anything with an exact check. There a checker makes the small model reliable, and this teaches the model to need the checker less. It does not fit judgement, writing, or anything a program cannot check: with no check there is no verified working to teach from, and nothing here would run.

For each role
  • Chief executive. The model your teams use gets better at your work, on your own hardware, and it never changes without a measurement and a named person's yes. Every change is on the record with their words.
  • Finance. not measured yet There is no charge per seat or per question in any of it: the costs are the machine you already own and the time it runs.
  • Operations. not measured yet Where an answer must be right, the checker stays in the loop exactly as before; this only reduces how often it has to rescue the model.
  • Technology and security. Everything runs on one machine: the model, the harvest, the training, the test. The training file is plain text and every line of it can be verified by anyone with a calculator. The weights it starts from are named by hash, and so is every adapter it makes.
  • Risk and compliance. A change to a model is the thing regulators and auditors ask about. Here each change has a written test set before it ran, a pass mark fixed in advance, the full training data, the before and after on unseen problems, the decision by code, and the sign-off by a person. All of it can be decided again from the files.
Against what you use now

A hosted model changes when its supplier changes it, and you are told afterwards if at all. Fine-tuning services ask for your data and return a model you cannot inspect being made. not measured yet What this cannot match is breadth: a large hosted model does far more kinds of work. This is for the narrow, checkable work you want to own.

Rolling it out
  1. Pick one kind of work with an exact check. Write the check first; it is the asset.
  2. Set aside a held-out block and never touch it.
  3. Run the checked process wide on the rest and keep only what verified.
  4. Train, test cold on the held-out block, and let the rule decide.
  5. Name the person who grants a change. Keep their words with the weights' hash.
  6. Stop when a round fails its bar. Report that as the result.

Risks, answered straight
  • "Will it get worse instead of better?" It can, and then the rule dissolves the weights and the base stays. not measured yet
  • "Is it just memorising?" The claims are measured only on problems that were never harvested and never trained on, and the page counts any overlap. not measured yet
  • "Does it still make confident mistakes?" Yes. not measured yet Keep the checker wherever a wrong answer costs you.
  • "Can it run away and keep changing itself?" No. It cannot start a round from weights nobody granted, it stops when a round fails, and it stops at a fixed number of rounds and hours.
  • "Does it work for our problems?" Only where you can write an exact check. That is a real limit and the first thing to find out.

The case for the board. The risk in owning a model is that it changes in ways nobody measured. Here nothing changes without a sealed test, a pass mark set in advance, and a named person's yes, and a change that fails is thrown away and published. not measured yet

White paper: verified self-distillation, one measured round at a time

The problem. A small model can be made reliable by a checker that tests every step and throws out the wrong ones. But the reliability belongs to the checker. The model is no better afterwards, and every answer costs many checked steps. The obvious fix, training the model on its own output, is known to make models worse: their errors are fed back as truth.

The idea. Train it only on output that passed the check. The checker already sorts the model's steps into verified and not. Keep the verified working of problems the model finished, throw everything else away, and train on that. Then measure the thing that matters: is the model better without the checker, on problems it has not seen, and no more confidently wrong?

The design. Six claims and their bars are written down first. The held-out problems are fixed by seed and struck from every training pool. Every file that can change a result is hashed into a seal before the round runs. The training file is verified again, line by line, by code that does not trust the harvest. Both models answer the same held-out questions under the same limits. Code applies the rule. A person grants or withholds the change. Then the whole round can be decided again from its records by anyone.

The result. not measured yet

The loop. Because one round is a sealed unit, rounds can be chained: an adopted and granted model becomes the next base. Gains should shrink round by round. The loop is built to stop where they stop, and to report that as its finding. not measured yet

What it does not show. That this works for other models or other kinds of problem. That the distilled model is safe to use unchecked. That the gains continue. That a model can improve itself without limit: this one is bounded by its check, its size, its compute and a person.

The one rule. Only verified work trains the model, only a held-out measurement changes it, and only a person lets the change stand.
